# Harbor Desk interaction contract

## Basis and delegation

This document proposes operator behavior for the fictional brief; it supplies no implemented capability. **Fixture facts** are the supplied identifiers, revision/permission validation, action lookup, correction evidence, best-effort cancellation and source-access limits. All presentation, focus and enablement rules below are **original project proposals**. Open service/product inputs are listed last. Preserve the team's existing visual system and stack.

Before delegation, show the selected sessions, room-change inputs, intended notice recipients and drafting objective. Explain that the assistant drafts; an authorized operator decides each publish proposal. Corrections affect remaining unsent drafts, and cancellation cannot promise stopped execution or recall. Changing the session scope requires deliberate review of affected work. Do not imply learned preferences or model confidence.

## Find and inspect a decision

Provide a persistent request list, including locally hidden notifications, with session, proposed room change, request identity, current attention state and last observation time. Distinguish “Needs review,” “Decision pending,” “Resolved,” “Superseded” and “Expired” from run activity and publish outcome. Several sessions remain independently inspectable; offer no bulk approval.

Opening a request shows its run/request IDs, immutable proposal/version, expected session revision, notice text, target session, attendee-segment snapshot and publish consequence. Compare old/new room information beside the proposed wording. Show segment scope and available recipient information without implying a live recipient count from a snapshot. Label the session-change record, segment snapshot and generated explanation separately, each with source and observation time. Link authorized underlying evidence; mark missing, stale or contradictory information. The generated explanation is rationale, not corroboration. If recipient access is later withdrawn, remove protected detail and show the access limitation while retaining permitted references.

“Publish notice” authorizes only this complete proposal/version against this session revision. Keep the effect and affected segment beside that action. Material changes require a refreshed comparison and deliberate new review. Do not silently carry approval to a replacement request.

## Edit, decline and changing authority

“Edit draft” opens a working copy with its base version. “Save revised draft” proposes a new version; publication requires reviewing the resulting immutable proposal. Preserve typed text during errors and conflicts. Acknowledgement of submitted version A must not erase later edit B. Show unsaved/saving/saved-for-version/error distinctly. Leaving a dirty editor offers “Keep editing” or “Discard changes”; explain that reload recovery is unavailable until draft retention is specified.

“Decline request” supplies no publish authorization and does not cancel other drafting. Keep the request pending until authoritative resolution is known. Dismissing its notification only hides that notification locally; “All requests” remains a route back and refreshes current status.

On expiry, supersession, permission loss or another operator's resolution, retain the inspected version and local edits, expose the authorized current snapshot and explain why the old actions are unavailable. Do not replay edits or a decision against a new request. Show any known resolving decision separately from downstream effect evidence. Service validation remains authoritative even after a fresh inspection.

## Publish and recover

Before dispatch, acknowledge retention of the service-generated action ID and minimal authorized request/proposal/version/session-revision references. If retention fails, keep publish unavailable and explain the recovery prerequisite. Keep the immutable submitted snapshot separate from subsequent edits. Guard repeated activation while this action is unresolved.

Show “Requested; delivery unconfirmed” after local dispatch, “Accepted; publication pending” only on matching service evidence, “Published” only on confirmed effect evidence, and “Rejected” with its established scope. Lost replies or inconclusive status produce “Publication outcome unknown,” with the original action reference, last checked time and “Check status.” Leaving ends neither the action nor its uncertainty.

On reload, recover the retained identity, reauthorize lookup and reconcile it before enabling an overlapping publish. Missing results, denied lookup or expired history remain unknown unless service evidence establishes otherwise. Preserve confirmed history when a later refresh fails. Do not offer automatic effect retry or a fresh overlapping submission while uncertain; the fixture supplies no duplicate/retry guarantee. A return summary links confirmed notice changes, outstanding requests, submitted corrections/cancellation commands and unresolved actions, and distinguishes current refresh failures from historical effects.

## Steer remaining drafting and cancel

The correction editor names affected remaining sessions and warns that earlier work may finish before application. Preserve editable text separately from the submitted correction identity/version/scope. Show “Sending,” “Receipt acknowledged,” and evidence identifying which later draft revisions used it. Acknowledgement alone never becomes “Applied.” Mark non-application only with supporting evidence; otherwise applicability remains unconfirmed. Lost replies leave receipt/application unconfirmed; retain the submitted snapshot, avoid automatic resubmission and block overlapping correction submission until a supported reconciliation rule is established. Later subject-matching text alone proves nothing.

“Request cancellation” explains its best-effort scope before activation. Show requested, acknowledged and task-record-closed separately from execution evidence. Neither acknowledgement nor record closure means work stopped. Retain any later notice/effect evidence beside the cancellation history. Offer no recall or rollback.

## Focus, ownership and enablement

Deliberate inspection focuses the request heading; edit entry focuses the notice field; return from editing focuses the revised comparison heading. Closing details returns to the originating request, or the list heading if it disappeared. Dirty-exit confirmation initially focuses “Keep editing”; choosing it restores editing focus. A failed deliberate save focuses a persistent error summary linked to affected fields; service/permission errors do not mark valid text invalid.

Background arrivals, expiry, conflict and completion preserve focus, selection and reading position. Keep an unavailable action discoverable with its reason and guarded against activation. Announce concise material status changes without moving focus; retain details outside transient messages and offer deliberate navigation to new requests. Do not announce every drafting event.

The service owns request resolution, authority, revisions and effect evidence; the interface owns editable intent and attention presentation. **Unresolved inputs:** product/service owners must define draft-save/decline outcomes and recovery, correction lookup/ordering, permission-safe retention and expiry, action-history lookup authorization, observation pacing and reconciliation ownership. Gate affected save/decline/correction recovery and reload-safe publish promises until those contracts are verified. No endpoints or storage technology are selected.

Future validation should trace concurrent resolution, stale review, lost replies, retention failure, denied evidence, correction races, return/reload and cancellation with later effects. Test keyboard/focus/announcements, content growth and operator understanding in supported environments. This document records proposed behavior, not executed UI, service, accessibility or user-study results.
