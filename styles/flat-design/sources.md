## Definition, practice and original research

| Primary source | Author and verified date | Limited role and reuse status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Flat Design: Its Origins, Its Problems, and Why Flat 2.0 Is Better for Users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-design/) | **Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group**, **27 September 2015**. | Authored definition and historical interpretation; distinguishes fully flat rendering from semiflat approaches. Historical explanation is credited, not treated as proof of a sole inventor. |
| [Flat-Design Best Practices](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-design-best-practices/) | **Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group**, **12 March 2017**. | Authored practical critique of weak action cues and a source of questions to assess. Atlas does not import its examples, pictures or checklist. |
| [Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/) | **Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group**, **3 September 2017**. | Primary eyetracking research report, with 71 participants and nine paired page stimuli. The comparison concerned stronger/weaker interaction signifiers in narrowly directed finding tasks. |
| [Response to Criticisms of Flat-Design Eyetracking Study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-criticisms-flat-design/) | **Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group**, **15 October 2017**. | Author's clarification of study scope. Absence of depth alone was not the isolated experimental variable. |

The experiment's reported **22% longer looking time** applies to its weak-signifier conditions and tasks. It is not a measured penalty for every flat interface or a forecast for a complete product workflow. Atlas performs no comparable human study and claims no measured efficiency improvement. This distinction is important when deciding whether clear two-dimensional controls can serve a task.

NNGroup's [Terms and Conditions — Articles and Videos](https://www.nngroup.com/contents/terms-and-conditions/) was inspected with its visible **2 September 2026** update date. It identifies NNGroup copyright and reserved rights, and describes its own limited quotation/reproduction policy. It is not a blanket open-source content license. Atlas provides original synthesis and attribution links; it does not redistribute article text, stimuli, templates or visuals.

## Authored system precedent and comparison

[Designing and building user interfaces for Windows](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2013/08/13/designing-and-building-user-interfaces-for-windows/), **13 August 2013**, has a **Windows Apps Team** byline and is signed **Moneta Ho Kushner, Senior Design Lead, Windows**. It is Microsoft's contemporary account of Windows 8.1 Preview design and describes composition, typography, color, patterns and platform behavior. It explicitly challenges a uniform-rectangles-only reading of that language. Credit belongs to Microsoft and the identified author/team; the date is this publication's date, not the invention of flat design. No permissive article or artwork license was verified; Atlas imports no Windows screens, logos, fonts, assets or component implementation.

Google / Android Developers' [Material Design 3 in Compose](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/designsystems/material3), visibly updated **22 October 2026 UTC**, describes both tonal and shadow elevation. It supplies a current primary comparison for a system that is broader than this strict flat recipe. Material authorship remains Google's; the reference is not a Material implementation or a claim that all its components require shadows. Its linked [Content License](https://developer.android.com/license) distinguishes documentation and other content with exceptions; no prose, diagrams, Compose code or assets are copied here.

It describes the color-scheme and forced-color model. W3C documents retain their own linked copyright, document-use and license notices; no standards prose, diagrams or code examples are imported.

Native labels, fieldsets, selects, checkboxes and buttons provide the platform semantics; Atlas authors the task behavior. The standard's own rights apply to its text and examples; none is imported.
